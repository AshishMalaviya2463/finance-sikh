When considering health insurance for your parents over the age of 60 it is important to consider all aspects of the policy. There can be waiting periods, co-payments, room limits, disease sub-limits, exclusions and pre-existing conditions clauses. These factors can affect how much you will pay out of your own pocket. Even though the policy seems cheap, it can be expensive.

## Consider

Start by thinking about what types of medical treatment you may require. What cover already do you have? What treatment are they currently undergoing and what is the financial capability of your family? Not everyone will be accepted onto the same policy at the same rate due to underwriting, medical history, geographical location, product design and insurance company guidelines. The official proposal and policy documents will dictate what is offered.

Who will be managing the administration? Older policy holders may feel more comfortable having their child or trusted relative helping them complete online tasks. However, you should ensure that they have the policy holder’s permission. You should also know how authorisation works. Ensure you have all policy numbers, customer services details and key documents so that you have them available during a hospital stay.

## Waiting Periods & Pre-existing Conditions

Waiting periods mean that you will not be covered for certain claims/conditions. Different products will offer different waiting periods, such as general waiting periods, waiting periods for certain diseases/procedures and waiting periods for pre-existing diseases. Ensure you know exactly what is covered and for how long by referring to your policy schedule and policy wording.

Ensure you accurately declare any medical history. Even if a condition is being managed with medication or hasn’t caused a recent hospitalisation, you shouldn’t assume it is irrelevant. If it is a question on the application form be sure to fully answer it to avoid any arguments. Ask the insurance company how they will manage the condition and keep a copy of their written reply.

## Co-pay

Co-pay means that you will pay a certain percentage of the eligible claim. This can vary between different policies. If there is a co-pay within your policy it is important to know whether it is a general co-pay, age band, certain hospitals and/or certain situations. Don’t assume based on the sales brochure.

This example is for illustrative purposes only. If a policy has a 20% co-pay on a claim of ₹1,00,000 it would mean you would pay ₹20,000 of the claim. This figure doesn’t take into consideration any deductibles, exclusions or sub-limits. This is purely an example. Real claims will depend on the policy and how the insurance company decide to manage the eligible costs.

## Room Limits & Sub-limits

Some policies will have a limit on what room you can claim for. If you choose a room that exceeds the limit outlined in your policy wording, you should know how this will affect your claim. Some policies will reduce any costs proportionally. How this is managed can vary so ask the insurance company how this will affect your claim. Keep a copy of their written response.

Sub-limits can apply to various treatments, procedures or costs. Some policies will have a limit on how much they will pay for a certain treatment. Even if your sum insured is higher than this amount, they will only pay up to the stated amount. Be sure to carefully review the detailed schedule.

## Pre-existing Conditions, Renewals & Premiums

Your parent may suffer from Diabetes, Hypertension or another condition that needs to be managed. Depending on what you declare, how they are underwritten, what the policy states and the regulations, you won’t know what will happen. This can’t be determined in a general article. Be sure to check the current IRDAI regulations and policy wording.

It is important to know how your policy will renew. This is to ensure you have cover. Find out how the premium is charged, when it needs to be paid, the grace period and what will happen if you don’t renew. Premiums can vary depending on the policy and regulations. Don’t assume that if you are quoted a price for age 61 you will pay the same price throughout the rest of your life. ## Exclusions & Claims Process

Policies can have various exclusions. Ensure you know what is excluded. Some policies will not cover consumables, non-medical items, outpatient, domiciliary, ambulance charges or certain procedures. The exact exclusions will vary. Don’t assume that because it says comprehensive cover it will cover everything.

Cashless can vary depending on the insurance company/authorised claims administrators and policy wording. It is important to know what you will have to pay for. You may have to pay for anything that isn’t approved. Keep a copy of your discharge summaries, prescriptions, bills, investigations and correspondence. Refer to the insurance company’s current list of documents they will require and deadlines.

## Premium affordability

Consider how much you are likely to be able to afford to pay for the premium. Choosing a policy that will be expensive to pay for can cause the policy to lapse when it is most valuable. However, don’t just look at the price, there may be limitations which increase out of pocket costs. Consider the whole package including premium, co-pay, room limits, sub-limits, exclusions, waiting periods and claims process.

If you have cover through your employer or another policy ensure you know how they will coordinate. Don’t assume that you will get double payment for the same expense. It will depend on what is stated in the contracts and regulations.

## Checklist

Review your policy schedule, customer information sheet, policy wording and exclusions. Know what your sum insured, co-pay, room cover, disease sub-limits, waiting periods, pre-existing conditions, renewal and claims process are. If you don’t understand any terms ask for clarification. Keep a copy of the written response from the insurance company.

Hospitals can vary in whether they are in the network. Ensure you know what the current status is. Have a realistic budget for any costs that may not be claimable. Insurance can help move some of the risk but not all costs will be covered and there may be issues when claiming.

## Summary

When looking at health cover for your parents over the age of 60 it is important to consider the terms of the contract. Waiting periods, co-pay, room limits, exclusions and renewal can all have a big impact on what you pay. Ensure you review the official policy documents, check current IRDAI regulations and that the premium is something you can afford. This guide is for educational purposes only and is a checklist.

## Planning for a planned hospital stay

Review the pre-approval process from your insurance company and contact the official claims channel. Find out what documents will be required. Ensure you know what the current status of the hospital is. Just because they are in the network doesn’t mean you will get cashless approval. The insurance company will still use the policy wording and will review your request. Have a back-up plan in case you have to pay for anything.

In the event of an emergency you may not have time to review all the clauses. However, if you have the policy schedule, customer services number and ID documents to hand this can save you unnecessary delays. Ensure your trusted relative knows where they can find the documents. Keep a copy of your itemised bills, prescriptions, investigations and discharge summary once you have left the hospital.

## Renewal

Before you renew your policy ensure you understand what your sum insured, exclusions, co-pay and room cover are. If the insurance company offer you the option to change, migrate or port, ensure you know what the conditions are. How will continuity be managed? Don’t let your policy lapse whilst you are considering your options. The consequences can vary depending on the policy and regulations. A new application may be underwritten differently.

## Coordination with family

Ensure whoever is helping you with the claim knows who they can talk to and how consent will be obtained. Keep copies of your pre-approval requests, discharge documents, prescriptions, diagnostics and itemised invoices. If the hospital ask for an upfront payment ask what is an estimate and what has been approved. Approval can vary depending on the final bill and policy wording. Don’t assume that you will be paid for everything.

Once you have left the hospital compare the settlement statement to your bill. Ask the insurance company what clause has been used to reduce your claim. If your claim has been declined/partially approved use the grievance process. Keep a copy of their response. Refer to current official guidance from IRDAI on escalation and timescales. Don’t refer to an old forum answer.

## Network

Even though it is convenient to have a local network hospital, don’t forget to check room limits, treatment sub-limits, exclusions and co-pay. Hospitals can change their network status. Ensure you know what the current status is and have a realistic way to pay for any costs that aren’t covered. The aim is to not rely on one aspect of marketing and forget about the other limitations in the contract.
