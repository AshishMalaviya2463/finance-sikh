Often life insurance can be viewed as one product, however there are a number of different products available. Term life insurance is typically a pure product offering life cover for a specific period of time. Savings life insurance products often include some form of life cover and a saving element which will depend on the terms of the policy. When looking at life insurance products you may have to look at the terms and conditions of each product to fully understand what they are offering.

## Purpose of the product

Life insurance is to provide the named beneficiaries with a payout if they die whilst the policy is active. The amount they receive is usually referred to as the sum assured however this will depend on the terms and conditions of the policy. The money received can be used by your family to recover lost income, pay off debts or for future costs however the amount of cover will not be determined by the contract.

Savings life insurance products may provide some benefit at maturity if the person lives up to the maturity date. This may be guaranteed, non-guaranteed or dependant on declared bonuses. It can often be wrongly assumed that all values shown in the sales illustration are guaranteed, however you should check that there are clear references to what is guaranteed within the contract.

## Cost of Premiums

The premium will depend on many factors such as underwriting, product, level of cover, length of cover, age and more. Term products tend to have lower premiums than savings life insurance products as they do not include any accumulation or maturity element. However features, terms and conditions will vary so you should not compare quotations unless they are for the same level of cover.

Just because a policy has a low premium it doesn’t mean it offers great value and vice versa. You should look at the terms and conditions to understand when you will receive a payout, what conditions apply and the impact of stopping payments. Understanding if there is a grace period, what happens if the policy lapses, what the revival terms are and if there are reduced paid up benefits can be very important.

## Liquidity

Generally speaking term insurance isn’t a saving product and you won’t get your premium back if you change your mind. If they do offer a return of premium this will be a specific feature of the contract so you should read carefully. Savings products may have a surrender or paid up clause however if you cancel your policy early you may receive a significantly different value. You should not assume your policy is a source of cash.

Ask what the surrender terms are and how the value will change. After a short period of time you may not have as much as you expect. Surrendering your policy will also mean you lose the cover. If you need your savings to be liquid you should understand the difference between the money you have saved in your insurance contract and what you can withdraw from a bank account.

## Terms and Conditions

All insurance products will have terms and conditions. These will vary between policies so you shouldn’t assume generic answers. The proposal form will also be important. Be honest and thorough when answering the questions relating to your health, smoking, occupation, existing policies and more. If you answer these incorrectly you may make claiming complicated in the future. You should keep a copy of your proposal form, medical information and correspondence with the insurer.

Look at the free look, cancellation, grace period, revival and intimation terms. Check what the current regulations are by referring to IRDAI and the official terms. [CONFIRM: current regulations regarding disclosure, free look period, grace periods and claims for the policy you are describing.]

## Guaranteed vs Assumed values

If it’s a savings product ask the insurer what is guaranteed and what is assumed. Look at when the premium will be paid, when the benefits will be paid, what happens if they die during different years of the policy, what the maturity terms are, what happens if you surrender and what happens to the bonuses. The maturity value shown will be a single value so you should look at when you’ll receive and what the terms are.

If it’s a term product understand what happens if they die, how long the term is, when the premium will be paid, if there are renewal or conversion terms, and if the payout is a lump sum. Riders may affect the terms and cost. Don’t just look at the single line premium.

## Tax implications

Depending on the type of policy, premium, date of issue, terms and conditions and the tax regime, tax implications can vary. Don’t assume all premiums are deductible and all maturities are tax free. Don’t enter into a long term contract purely because of the tax benefits. Tax rules can change and you will need to consider the cost, liquidity and benefits. Refer to official documentation from the Income Tax Department and seek professional advice from a qualified tax professional.

## Key Questions

What triggers the payout, who will receive it, how long will you be paying the premium, what happens if you miss a premium, what is guaranteed, how much will you receive if you surrender early, are there any exclusions or waiting periods, how will you make a claim and what documents may you need? This information should be available in official documentation.

You should also consider if you can afford to pay the premium if you change jobs, become ill or have family expenses. If you can no longer afford to pay your policy may lapse. If you have a long term policy you should consider doing a realistic cashflow to see if you can afford it. If you have other major goals within your family this should be considered.

## Common Misconceptions

Term insurance is not a liquid investment. Savings insurance is not the same as a bank account or mutual fund. The maturity value may not be guaranteed and can include assumed values. Tax implications will vary between policies and tax payers. You should understand the product based on the terms.

## Summary

There are often big differences between term and savings life insurance products. Look at the actual contract, guaranteed values, non-guaranteed values, surrender terms, exclusions and premium commitment. Confirm the current tax and regulatory terms from official sources. This will help you understand the features of the product. It does not recommend any particular policy or how much cover your family will need.

## Nomination and Family Communication

It can be difficult to manage insurance within your family if no-one knows about your policy or where the documents are kept. Keep a personal record of which insurers you have, policy numbers, when you need to pay and where your policy is. Let the right family member know how to find the information without revealing any sensitive data. Review your nomination details if you get married, separated, have a baby, pass away or have another major change within your family.

The legal implications of having a nominee can vary depending on the situation and law. Don’t assume that having a nominee will solve any issues with inheritance or ownership. If you have a complex family structure it may be appropriate to seek professional legal advice. You should still read the policy to understand how to make a claim and what documents may be required.

## Can I afford it?

Consider if you can afford to pay the premium if your income drops temporarily or you have another major expense. As this is a long term product you shouldn’t just consider the initial premium. If you miss a payment it will depend on the grace, lapse and revival terms of your policy. Read these terms in advance and ask for written clarification if they are unclear.

## Changing Circumstances

Your life rarely remains the same throughout the term of your insurance. You may take out a new loan, have a child, change jobs or take care of a parent. Consider if you still want the same level of cover and premium commitment. However you should read the terms of your policy. Cancelling your policy could leave you without cover and you may have to take out a new policy which may involve new underwriting.

Ask your insurer what changes can be made to your policy, can the sum assured be changed and what are the terms for converting, paid up or surrendering. Don’t assume that what your agent tells you verbally will be a benefit in your contract. Keep written confirmation and compare to your policy. The purpose of reviewing is to understand your options and what will happen.

## Don’t compare dissimilar cashflows

If comparing term and savings products ensure the level of cover, term and premium payment are as similar as possible. Ensure you know what is guaranteed. If one product shows maturity value and the other doesn’t they aren’t the same. Remember the money you pay in as premium can’t be used for anything else during the term. A fair comparison would be to understand the cashflow and terms.
