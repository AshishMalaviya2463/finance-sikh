The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a small savings scheme that is backed by the Government. Unlike a bank savings account your money will be invested for a long period of time and will be subject to the rules of the scheme. It is also different to a market linked mutual fund as the PPF account follows the rules of the notified scheme and therefore the interest rate can vary dependent on declaration by the Government. When researching a PPF you should look at the difference between the interest rate and the rules of the scheme.

## PPF Structure & Eligibility

This account can be opened in the name of an eligible individual either via an authorised bank or post office. The rules state who can open the account, if there can be joint names on the account, how the account operates for minors and that you cannot open more than one account in the same name. You should look at the latest official documentation for the rules of the scheme. There may be additional steps required by the banks/post offices for you to complete to ensure you meet the requirements for opening the account such as confirming your identity, nominating and making deposits.

The PPF account is designed for long term savings. You will make deposits into the account and interest will be added using the interest calculation method from the scheme. As the timing of the deposits may affect the interest, do not assume that you can make deposits on any day of the year. The latest official guidance will tell you what the calculation method is and what the timing requirements are.

## Minimum and Maximum Deposits

The official documentation for the scheme will state what the minimum deposit per year is and the maximum amount that can be contributed to the account. This should be checked to ensure the correct information is known as these are facts and could change. If you are unable to meet the minimum deposit requirement the account could be affected and there may be a process to reactivate the account and/or charges applied based on the rules. Do not rely on information from an old blog or screenshot.

Your deposits should be planned to ensure they work with your cash flow and you are not needing to borrow or ignore any immediate payments. You can choose to make a monthly deposit, however the PPF scheme may allow different deposit patterns. The timing should take into consideration your salary dates, annual costs and how much cash you have available outside of the account. The PPF is not a substitute for emergency cash.

## Length of Investment & Extending the Account

The PPF is intended to be a long term investment however there are rules in place regarding extending the account. The maturity date will impact when you can access the funds and what options you have. The process for extending the account and how any additional contributions will be dealt with should be checked against the latest official rules. Do not assume all extension options will have the same impact.

When opening an account you should consider how long the funds will be tied up for and whether this works with your reasons for opening the account. If you need the money for a deposit on a house, a business cost or school fee before the maturity date then you may need to hold funds elsewhere. Whilst having a long investment period can encourage you to save for the future it can also have less flexibility. Ensure you know what the account will be used for before making large deposits into it.

## Interest Rate Declarations

The interest rates are declared by the Government for small savings schemes for specific periods of time. The interest rate shown on the official page is a declaration for the period and is not guaranteed to be the rate for the whole life of your new account. You can be misled if you calculate using the rate for 15 years as this is an example.

Using an online calculator you should check if the calculator assumes a fixed rate. If so then this is an example. More carefully, you should note what rate was used and when it was checked. Future declarations may vary. The actual balance in the account will be based on the rules and rates.

## Withdrawing & Loans

The PPF has specific rules for when partial withdrawals can be made, how much can be withdrawn and if there is an option for loans during a specific period. The account will need to be a certain age and meet the conditions of the scheme. It is not a regular savings account and you should not assume you can withdraw at any time you need to pay for something. You should check the latest rules with the official scheme and the institution.

Having restrictions on accessing your funds can be both a positive and a negative. If you have an irregular income, dependents or high medical exposure you may want to have a separate cash fund. Having your emergency cash tied up in a long term account can cause problems with timing.

## Tax Status

For Tax Year 2026-27, eligible PPF deposits can be included in the aggregate deduction under section 123 read with Schedule XV of the Income-tax Act, 2025, up to ₹1.5 lakh across the qualifying payments listed there. This deduction is available under the old regime, subject to the schedule’s conditions; it is not available under the default new regime in section 202.

Interest credited to a PPF account and qualifying payments from the account receive the exemption provided under the PPF scheme and the applicable income-tax provisions. Do not extend that statement to every transaction without checking the account’s compliance with the scheme, the nature of the receipt and the current law. PPF’s tax treatment is often described as exempt contribution, exempt interest and exempt qualifying maturity proceeds, but the contribution deduction and the exemption on interest/withdrawal are separate legal questions.

For the contribution deduction, read [section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-123-92) and Schedule XV. For the scheme’s account and deposit conditions, read the [Public Provident Fund Scheme, 2019](https://nsiindia.gov.in/InternalPage.aspx?Id_Pk=169). Check the latest Income-tax Department guidance before filing a return.

Not all taxpayers will find it beneficial to get a tax deduction. Depending on the regime you may not be able to claim it or it may not be relevant. You should not make a contribution purely to get a tax tag on the account. You should always refer to the official Income Tax Department guidance.

## Drawbacks

The most obvious downside is the long investment period, however there are several others. The interest rate can change, the account can be restrictive and it isn’t as exposed to the market as an equity investment. Just because it is Government backed doesn’t mean it will be suitable for all purposes. When comparing to other savings you should look at the purpose and rules.

There can also be administration issues. Ensuring the details are up to date, deposits have been added to the account, keeping statements, knowing what your nominations are and ensuring any service is completed correctly. A small error when making deposits can cause problems when trying to access your funds at maturity/withdrawal. Keep records and use the official process with your institution.

## Common Misconceptions

The PPF is not a daily account. Do not assume the interest rate will stay the same for the whole investment period. Do not assume you will automatically be able to extend your account on your preferred terms. Do not assume the tax status will be the same as in an article before 2026. A long term account can be difficult to change once you have deposited your funds.

## Summary

The PPF is a long term small savings scheme with set rules on contributions, maturity and withdrawal. It may be suitable for some long term savings purposes however you should be aware of the lack of liquidity and varying interest rates. You should check the latest scheme documentation for rules, check what the conditions are for making deposits and withdrawing and check the tax status. This is an explanatory guide outlining what the account is like. Whether it is suitable for your household will depend on your circumstances.

## Administration for Long Term Account

As the account will run for a long period of time you may have moved house, changed your phone number, bank or family circumstances. Ensure your contact details are updated, check your nominations and store your account details securely. If you are moving your account between an authorised bank and post office you should follow the current official process and keep evidence of your request. Never give away your passwords, PIN or one time passwords to someone claiming to help you with an everyday update.

Before your account reaches maturity check what the official process is and what the deadline is for requesting an extension or withdrawal. Do not leave it until the last week to find out you need to fill in forms, sign and confirm your identity. The exact process can vary per institution so you should check the current written guidance.

## Consideration for Household

You should think about what your immediate cash needs are, other savings and any long term commitments. If you have a large balance in a locked account you may still struggle to pay for something unexpected if you don’t have an emergency cash fund. Some people may like having a structured long term account and intentionally keep some money separate for shorter term. Knowing what the purpose of the account is will make it clearer.

Don’t be confused by the Government backed scheme. This doesn’t mean you will get the same rate and rules in the future. The account will operate based on the legal terms and official declarations. You should double check these when making a big decision.

## Double Check Information

If you are seeing different info in your bank app, post office passbook and online calculator firstly check the dates and that all your deposits have been added. The projection may be using a fixed rate whereas your actual account will show the rate applicable for the periods. Check your transaction ledger and official interest rate declaration. If you still can’t understand why there is a difference you should contact your institution using their official process and keep your complaint reference.

You should be aware of the difference between the maximum deposit and maximum balance. If there is a maximum deposit per year based on the rules of the scheme this does not mean there is a maximum balance. The current official rules will tell you what the maximum is and what will happen if you exceed it. Do not assume based on what a calculator says or what someone else says.

## Build a contribution habit without stretching cash flow

PPF is a long-term account, so the contribution plan should be sustainable. You can deposit in one payment or in instalments, subject to the scheme's annual minimum and maximum. Before choosing a monthly amount, account for rent, EMIs, insurance premiums, school fees and emergency savings. A contribution that forces you to borrow on a credit card defeats the purpose of disciplined saving. If your income is irregular, you may prefer to make contributions when cash is available while keeping track of the annual minimum and deadline.
