# Gold rates in India


The rate of gold can vary throughout the day.
Understanding how the rate is calculated will allow you to get an overview and compare the rates. You may notice that depending on what jewellers you go to the rates can vary between morning and evening.

### There is no single rate:

When you search for today’s gold rate you may see a rate per gram or 10 grams. This could be referring to:
- **International Rate** - This is the international rate and is usually measured in USD per Troy Ounce.
- **Domestic Benchmark** - This is a benchmark used in the Indian market. This is published by organisations like the [India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA)](https://ibjarates.com/index.aspx).
- **Exchange Rate** - This is the rate paid for the standardised futures contract. This will be on exchanges like the [Multi Commodity Exchange of India (MCX)](https://www.mcxindia.com/en/products/bullion/GOLD).
- **Local Bullion/Jewellery Rate** - This is the rate paid for the gold by the business. This can vary depending on local premiums or discounts.
- **Final Jewellery Rate** - This would include the purity, weight and any making/other charges plus taxes.
These are all related but cannot be compared directly. When comparing rates ensure you check the date, time, purity, units, location and taxes.

## International Rate

As gold is traded internationally the international rate will vary depending on what is being bought and sold. This is usually quoted in USD per Troy Ounce. 1 Troy Ounce is roughly 31.1035 Grams.

### Factors affecting the international rate:

- **Interest rates/bond yields** - As gold doesn’t earn interest higher bond yields may appear more favourable. Future interest rates can also be key.
- **US Dollar** - A strong dollar can make gold expensive in other currencies. However, a weak dollar can do the opposite.
- **Inflation/Risk** - Investors may invest in gold due to inflation uncertainty, geopolitical risk and financial instability. This does not guarantee a short term hedge.
- **Central Banks/Investment** - Central banks, gold backed funds, gold bars, coins and futures will affect the market.
- **Supply** - Mined and recycled gold.
This will be a major factor but may not be the final rate you pay in India.

## Rupee/Dollar

India imports a lot of gold. As most international gold is quoted in USD the USD/INR rate will be important.
If the international rate is stable but the rupee weakens against the dollar, the importer will pay more rupees to get the same amount of gold. If the rupee strengthens you’ll see little cost in rupees.
This can mean that you see the gold rate increasing in India even if the international rate isn’t moving.

**Rupee Value = International Value + USD/INR + Weight + Any relevant import and domestic costs.**

This is a simple example. Calculations will vary depending on the benchmark, import rules, duties, premiums and market.

## Import Costs & Domestic Factors

The rate you get for the imported gold is often known as the landed rate. This is generally calculated using the international value, converting to rupees and adding any relevant import duties, customs, logistics, finance and other relevant charges.
Changes to the customs duties or import rules by the government can affect this. How quickly this is reflected can depend on the amount of stock, contracts, premiums and competition.
If there is a local premium the domestic gold will be higher than the international landed rate. If there is a discount it will be lower. This depends on the demand, stock, import, finance and trading. This means that the prices in India may not increase/decrease by the same percentage as the international rate.

## IBJA Rate Calculation

The AM and PM rates are published by the [India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA)](https://ibjarates.com/index.aspx). These rates are often used as a domestic benchmark. They state that the rates are calculated by polling members of the physical gold chain. This would include bullion dealers, refiners, importers, scrap dealers, exporters and jewellers.
They state that on business days they will poll twice for tradable rates. Once at around 11.30am - 12pm and again at around 4.30pm - 5pm. These will be published at around 12.05pm and 5.05pm. These will be an average of rates excluding the highest and lowest rates. They also say that they will start with a base rate for 995 and calculate the 999 and other purities.
Ensure you check the latest publication by the IBJA to see if there have been any changes to the method or timing. The benchmark will not necessarily be published at the exact minute by all jewellers.
They also state that the rate includes import duties and customs but does not include GST and some other charges. This is not a full jewellery invoice.

## What is MCX?

The [Multi Commodity Exchange of India (MCX)](https://www.mcxindia.com/en/products/bullion/GOLD) is a regulated market where you can trade commodity derivatives including gold futures. Futures are standardised contracts with set details and an expiry date. The price will depend on market sentiment, hedging, liquidity and supply/demand.
The rates provided by MCX will give market participants an idea of domestic sentiment and help them manage their exposure. This will not necessarily be the final retail price for your necklace. Futures can differ to the spot benchmark as they have an expiry date, finance, storage, delivery and expectations. Ensure you check what contract you are looking at and what the time stamp is.
The IBJA benchmarks and MCX futures are similar but may not be the final selling rate in all shops.

## Different rates for 24K, 22K & 18K

Purity refers to how much gold is present. Karat is measured in 24.
- **24K** - This is very pure gold. Often you will see references to 999 and 995 depending on the product and benchmark.
- **22K** - This is 22/24 gold. This is roughly 91.6% gold. Often marked as 916.
- **18K** - This is 18/24 gold. This is 75% gold. Often marked as 750.

### The lower the Purity

The cheaper it will be per gram. 22K and 18K rates on websites may be sourced and calculated differently. Ensure you check how they are calculated.
Ensure you check the purity and hallmark of the gold you are buying. The [BIS](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-overview/hallmarking-faqs/hallmarking-faq/?lang=en) state that a gold hallmark shows how much precious metal is present in the item. Hallmarked jewellery will include the [BIS](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-overview/hallmarking-faqs/hallmarking-faq/?lang=en) logo, purity/fineness and a 6 character alphanumeric HUID. This can be checked using the [BIS Care App](https://www.bis.gov.in/) by using the Verify HUID option.

## Why is my jeweller in [city] giving me a different rate?

The national benchmark is a reference rate and may not be the rate you pay. The rates may vary in your area depending on the bullion supply and demand, stock, local premium, delivery, running costs, purity, time and shop policies.
Do not assume that just because there is a city mentioned that the national benchmark will be updated for that city. Ensure the rate states where it is from, the time, purity and what it includes.

## Jewellery Bill

The rate of gold will only be part of your jewellery bill. You may see making charges, wastage or other charges included plus any tax.

### Basic breakdown of jewellery bill:


**Gold Value = Net Weight x Purity Rate**

The jeweller will then add any charges and tax. This can vary depending on the product and jeweller. Ask for a breakdown.
Making charges can vary. This can be a fixed amount, percentage or calculated differently. This will cover the design and making costs. Ensure you check if the weight includes any stones, beads etc.
Ensure you compare offers using the same purity, net weight, making charges, tax, stone value, buyback and hallmark.

## Why do the rates vary throughout the day? Why may they not vary on holidays?

International markets may vary whilst shops are closed. Currency markets may vary. Domestic benchmarks and exchange contracts will have their own times. Some jewellers may update quicker than others once they receive a new rate. Websites will automatically update.
On weekends/holidays there may not be an update to the benchmark. If the website is showing the rate from the last business day ensure it states what date it is and that it is not a new rate for the holiday. Never show old data as being updated today.

## How can I check the gold rate?


### Quick checklist:

- **Time** - Check if it is today’s rate and whether it is AM/PM.
- **Source** - Check if it is an IBJA benchmark, MCX contract, local bullion or jeweller.
- **Purity** - 22K to 22K and 24K to 24K. Like grades.
- **Unit** - Per gram, 8 gram or 10 gram?
- **Includes** - Any import charges? Excludes GST? Any separate making charges?
- **Breakdown** - Ensure you know the net weight, purity, making charges, tax and any charges for stones etc.
- **Hallmark** - Check the purity and HUID. Keep your invoice.
This will help you avoid confusion between the benchmark and final rate.

For a reference on how a national benchmark differs from a jeweller's retail quote, see our [gold price page](/gold-price-today/).

## Summary

Gold rates in India will be affected by the international market, USD/INR, import costs/policies, local premiums/discounts and local factors. AM/PM benchmarks are published by the IBJA. These are calculated using a specific method. MCX will show futures contracts. Jewellers will set their retail rates depending on how they source their gold, stock, purity, charges and tax.
There is no single rate for all gold in all cities. Ensure you check the source, time, purity, unit and includes. Ask for a breakdown if you are buying jewellery.

## FAQs


### Do gold rates vary every minute in India?

International markets and exchange contracts may vary. The IBJA publish their benchmark using a specific method. They will poll twice on business days. Retail jewellers may vary on when they update.

### Is the IBJA rate the final rate I’ll pay?

No. The IBJA state that their benchmark does not include GST or some other charges. The jewellery may include making charges and other charges.

### Why can gold be expensive in India if the global rate is stable?

If the rupee is weak the import costs will be higher. This can depend on import costs and local premiums.

### Is MCX gold the same as 22K gold from a jeweller?

No. MCX gold is a futures contract on the exchange. This will have specific details. The jeweller will have rates depending on their retail purity, how they source their gold, local factors, charges and tax.

### What is the difference between 22K and 24K gold?

22K gold is roughly 91.6% gold. 24K references are very pure. Ensure you check the fineness and hallmark on the item.
