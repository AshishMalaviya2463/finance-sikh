This is a life insurance product. You will receive a benefit if you pass away within the term of the policy. Often this will be referred to using the term sum assured. However, this may be detailed in the policy. This is not an investment product therefore should not be directly compared to others using the premium.

## Understanding Cover

Within your policy, the term sum assured will be an important part. However, it will not represent what your family will actually need. Depending on your circumstances, such as any financial commitments (home loan, running costs, education costs, dependents), your savings, other cover and future earnings, this can impact how much cover you may need. This article will not tell you how much cover you should have but will explain why the figure given in the headline should be understood in the context of your circumstances.

## Term

You may have a term policy. This will mean that your cover will last for a set period of time. As your children grow older, debts reduce and you have more savings you may see a change in how much cover you need. If your policy lapses whilst you still have financial dependence then you may not have enough cover. Your schedule should detail when your cover begins and ends plus the premium required.

## Underwriting

The insurer will want you to provide some information. This can include your age, medical history, occupation, lifestyle factors and any existing cover. Medicals and additional information may be requested. The insurer will decide whether to accept, decline, charge, exclude or add other terms.

Make sure that all your proposal forms are filled in correctly. Don’t miss out any information even if it seems irrelevant or someone says it will give you a better quote. Keep a copy of your application form and any declarations. If your agent/intermediary has filled in the forms for you, make sure you have read through all the answers. This could affect your claim.

## Riders

These are additional benefits added to your policy. These can be a range of things from additional benefits if you pass away due to an accident, disablement or suffer from a particular illness. The names and benefits of riders can vary so it is important to understand what they are. This can be done by reading the wording in the policy. An additional premium may be charged. To qualify for the rider there may be conditions, exclusions and a separate claims process.

Don’t assume that everything relating to the name of the rider will be covered. The definition, medical proof, time frames and exclusions will all affect this. Try to obtain a written explanation of how your rider works. Is it an additional benefit? An accelerated benefit? Does it lapse before your main cover? ## Exclusions

These are situations where you will not receive the benefit within your policy. The wording will vary depending on your policy. Don’t make general statements like "All claims will be covered from...". This should only be stated if your policy and law allows you to. Read through the exclusions, conditions, definitions and documents required.

You should also understand what happens in terms of grace periods, lapses, revival and nominations. If you don’t pay your premium correctly then your policy may lapse. Make sure that your family have easy access to the insurers details and policy number. Beneficiaries may struggle to find the documents.

## Understanding Claims Data

There is lots of data available from insurers and regulators. The most popular being the claims ratio. This can be misleading as it depends what is in the denominator, how long the time frame is, what types of claims are included, if it is based on amount or numbers and if it includes pending claims. Always look at what the exact definition is in the official report.

A high ratio doesn’t mean that your claim will be accepted. A lower ratio doesn’t mean that your claim was not paid. This will depend on your policy, any disclosures, medical proof, exclusions and whether the claims process was followed. Claims data should be used as a general background only.

Don’t use the number from the article, make sure you have the source, time frame and what it is measuring. [CONFIRM BEFORE PUBLISHING: What is the latest claims data from IRDAI? What is the time frame? What is the definition? Any caveats?] If this isn’t present in the official report don’t guess.

## Beneficiaries and Claims

Nominations can help you understand who your beneficiary is. This will depend on the law and policy. The policyholder should ensure the details are correct. They should also update the details after a major life event. Don’t rely on nominations to understand the legal ownership and succession. Complex families may need advice.

Keep your schedule, proposal, premium receipts and correspondence with your insurer together in a place your family can easily find. Let them know where to look but don’t share any sensitive information. Your beneficiaries should know how to formally make a claim with the insurer, and what documents are generally required. The current claims process should be followed.

## Affordability

Whilst you have cover this will only be beneficial. If you lose your job, your income changes or your family have more commitments then this can become a burden. Before signing make sure that you can afford to pay for the premium. This shouldn’t affect your ability to pay for your home, debts, health etc. Check the dates and what payment options are available in the official terms.

Don’t cancel your existing cover without thinking. New cover may need to go through underwriting and won’t be on the same terms. If there are any changes make sure you understand when the new contract will begin and what happens to the old contract.

## Queries

What is the benefit? How long will my cover last? What is the premium? What will I need to declare? Are there any exclusions? Can I update my nominee details? How will claims work? Ensure you have the full wording of any riders. If there is a difference between what has been said in the sales information and in the policy ask the insurer to provide a written explanation.

## Conclusion

Term insurance relies on clear terms, correct disclosures and following the policy. Read through the sum assured, term, riders and exclusions. Claims data can only be used as a background and you should understand what it means and the time frame. It won’t tell you if your claim will be successful. Use current IRDAI information and official policy documents. This is a general explanation and should not be taken as a personal recommendation.

## Free Look/Grievance

Depending on the regulations your policy may have a free look/review period. Check what the conditions and time frames are in the current regulations and policy. As soon as you receive the policy read through it. Ensure that this is what you applied for. If there is a material difference between what has been explained and the actual policy contact your insurer formally.

Keep a note of your complaint number, any written communication and final response. IRDAI provide official information for policyholders. The process and timeframes can vary, link to current guidance. Never say that a claim will be guaranteed to have a specific outcome from a regulator.

## Sales Illustration Wording

Words such as guaranteed, projected, bonus and illustrated can all mean different things. Ask the insurer to highlight what is a contractual obligation and what will be declared in the future. Don’t rely on the colour of the chart. Keep a copy of your illustration.

## Claims File

The policy owner may not be the one making the claims. Keep your schedule, proposal, premium receipts, nomination details and insurer details together. A simple note of where they can be found will save time. Keep your file safe as it will contain personal and financial information. Ensure that the correct family member can find it.

Depending on your insurer and regulations you may be asked to provide ID, proof of death and other documents. This will vary. Use the current official claims checklist from your insurer. If there is a delay in claiming keep a note of the reference number and ask for the missing requirement in writing.

## Data

If there is a ratio at insurer level in the publication ensure you can locate the table. You should be able to replicate the definition. Policies or amounts? What is the time frame? Does it include all individual life? If you don’t have this information explain that you can’t fairly compare. Don’t rank the insurers based on one stat.
