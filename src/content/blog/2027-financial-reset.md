When looking to reset your finances for the new year, you don’t need to go out and invest in new products. Instead, think about whether your money habits, savings, cover and goals match your lifestyle.

Take an hour out to look at your statements and policies and go through these 7 key checkpoints. Depending on your income, dependents, debt, job security and goals will determine what you prioritise.

## 1. Budgeting & Cashflow

This is about understanding where your money is going. Look at your most recent 2-3 months of bank and card statements. Categorise your expenses into essential, discretionary and non-essential. Essential expenses could be rent, food, utilities, transport, school fees, medication and minimum loan repayments.

List out your non-monthly expenses like your annual insurance premium, car servicing, school fees & festival travel. Work out how much you will spend in a year and divide this by 12. By setting aside this amount, you can plan for predictable bills.

Are you able to cover your essential expenses with your income? Are you saving for your annual expenses? Are you saving a realistic amount for your savings & discretionary? If your income varies each month, it’s best to budget using a conservative estimate.

## 2. Emergency Savings

An emergency fund can help provide some financial security when you have unexpected essential expenses or if you lose income. This can allow you to deal with a career change, urgent repairs or medical costs without having to sell long-term investments or borrow at a high cost.

As a guide, most people aim to save 3-6 months of essential expenses. However if you have one income earner, dependents or earn an unpredictable income with large EMIs, you may want to save a little more.

For example, if your essential monthly expenses are ₹35,000, you would save ₹1,05,000 for 3 months and ₹2,10,000 for 6 months. This is just an example. Work out your essential expenses including minimum loan repayments and any costs that you would still have to pay even if you cut back on your non-essential expenses.

It’s important to make sure you can easily access your money. Before deciding where to put your money, think about any rules or risks associated with withdrawing it. Don’t invest in shares or equity Mutual Funds as they can lose value when you need to withdraw. Make sure you know what [deposit protection](https://www.dicgc.org.in/) applies.

If you don’t have any savings, start with a small target and set up a manageable automatic payment after your payday. If you do use your emergency fund, think about how much you should be aiming to save and create a realistic plan.

## 3. Health & Life Insurance

Just because you have insurance doesn’t mean you have sufficient cover.

With health insurance look at how much you are insured for, who is covered, any waiting periods, exclusions, co-payment clauses, maximum room rent and if you are covered for cashless or reimbursement. Read through all the policy wording and schedule. If you have employer provided insurance, think about what will happen if you leave your job.

With life insurance consider who depends on your income and what responsibilities you would have if your income stopped. Consider the policy term, how much you are insured for, exclusions, premium and nominee. Term insurance & health insurance are for different reasons.

Don’t cancel your policy just because it’s a new year. Think about whether you have the right cover, conditions and affordability. If you cancel your current cover and don’t have new cover in place you will have a gap. If you’re unsure ask your insurer to provide written confirmation.

## 4. High Interest Debt

Debt can impact how much you can save. List out all your loans/credit facilities, outstanding balance, interest rate, minimum payment, due date and any charges. This includes credit cards, personal loans, car loans etc.

Usually, paying the minimum payment on your credit card won’t clear the full balance. You may still be paying interest & charges depending on the terms. Look at your latest statement and agreement.

After you’ve paid your essential expenses and have a reasonable amount of cash in hand, look at the cost of borrowing. Paying off high interest debt can save you money in the future. However, look at any prepayment conditions and charges. Don’t use all your accessible savings as you may be likely to borrow again in an emergency.

Make sure you’re keeping up with the required payments. Any extra payments should go towards paying off the debt with the highest effective cost (taking fees and conditions into consideration). If you’re struggling to pay, get in touch with your lender as soon as possible.

## 5. Investments & Goals

Your investments should have a purpose. List out your main goals, when you might need the money and what investments you have for each goal. If you have a short term goal & retirement, you may need to approach this differently.

Look at your Mutual Funds, shares, deposits, provident fund & any other investments. Are you invested in a good mix for your goals and risk profile? Don’t invest too heavily in one company, sector or asset type as this could expose you to risks.

If you’re investing via SIPs, make sure they’re affordable and that they still fit the purpose. Don’t increase your SIP as the market has been performing well recently. Don’t stop your long-term investment as it’s dipped in the short term.

Look at the cost, documentation and asset allocation. Just because something has performed well in the past doesn’t mean it will in the future. Don’t make decisions purely based on social media recommendations or 1 year returns.

## 6. Tax Records

Make sure you’re tax planning is in line with your income and tax year. Organise your salary statements, bank interest, investment details, insurance documentation, home loan statements & any other relevant documents.

If you’re a salaried employee, look at the different tax regimes available. Make sure you know what deductions/exemptions you have. The new tax regime is the default but eligible taxpayers may have the option to choose another tax regime. Don’t assume something will be beneficial just because it says it’s tax saving.

For 2026-27 look at the current guidance from the Income Tax Department and Income-tax Act, 2025. Tax forms, deadlines & eligibilty can change. If you have freelance income, capital gains, rental income or multiple sources of income make sure you’re keeping track of each category carefully. You may want to consider getting professional help.

Keep your documents organised for each tax year. Make sure your records match those on the official tax portal. Keep evidence of any eligible payments but don’t share any sensitive documents with unknown websites or agents.

Official guidance can be found here: [Income Tax Department – e-Filing](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/).

## 7. Nominees, Account Details & Important Documents

Without knowing where your family can find your accounts, your financial planning won’t be complete. Make sure you know who your nominees are for your bank, fixed deposits, Mutual Funds & insurance. Make sure the names and details are correct, especially if you’ve had a major change in your family.

The nominee may not be the ultimate owner of the asset depending on the asset and succession laws. Nomination can help an institution process a claim but it won’t replace estate planning or a valid will if required.

Make sure your address, mobile number, email, bank details & KYC details are correct with the relevant institutions. Keep a secure record of your institutions, policy numbers, account references & customer service. Never save any passwords, PINs, full card details or OTPs in a standard document.

Let a trusted family member know where your essential documents are.

## Simple 30 Day Financial Reset Plan

- **Week 1** – Understand your cashflow. Look at your statements and list out your essential monthly expenses. Know what annual expenses you have coming up.
- **Week 2** – Build your safety net. Work out how much you should be saving for your emergency fund. Look at your health & life insurance documents.
- **Week 3** – Deal with your obligations & investments. List out your debts. Look at the cost of interest. Look at your goals. See if your investments make sense.
- **Week 4** – Organisation. Organise your tax records. Check your nominee & KYC details. Secure your important documents.

Write down 3 things you’ll do and when you’ll review them. It’s better to have a short list that you can stick to than a long list that you’ll forget by February.

## Common Mistakes

Don’t fall into common traps such as investing in something just to save tax, investing your emergency fund in volatile assets, ignoring high cost debt, cancelling your insurance without new cover in place, chasing recent returns and leaving your nominee details out of date.

## FAQs

### What should I do financially before 2027?

Start by looking at your budget, emergency fund, insurance, debt, investments, tax records & nominee details. Focus on any urgent gaps.

### How much should I have in my emergency fund?

Most people aim to have between 3-6 months of essential expenses saved. If you earn an unpredictable income, have dependents or large repayments you may need to save more.

### Should I increase my SIP in 2027?

Only increase if you have the income, cashflow, emergency fund & any other obligations to allow you to. Make sure it still fits with your goals and risk profile. Don’t increase your contributions just because it’s a new year.

### Should I invest to save tax before the end of the year?

First look at the tax regimes available and make sure you’re eligible. Don’t invest in something just to try and get a deduction. Look at the official tax guidance or get advice from a qualified tax professional.

### How often should I review my finances?

Try to review your cashflow each month and have a more detailed review each year. If you’ve had a career change, new dependents, loan or income change, review.

## Final Thoughts

A Financial Reset isn’t about becoming a millionaire overnight or ticking off someone else’s checklist. It’s about knowing what you have coming in, going out, what you owe, what is protecting your household and what your savings are for.

Focus on fixing the most important gaps. Keep your records organised. If your life changes, review your decisions. Making small regular improvements can make your finances easier to manage throughout 2027.
