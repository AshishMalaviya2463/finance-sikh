ELSS, PPF, and NPS often come up as a single unit in conversation. Whilst this isn’t an interchangeable product there are key differences between the products. ELSS is an equity based mutual fund scheme. PPF is a government small savings scheme with clear rules. NPS is a pension scheme with a regulated account and exit rules. There are key differences between the risk of the investments, limitations on access to the funds and the tax implications.

## Understanding the structure of the product

ELSS is an Equity Linked Savings Scheme (mutual fund). This means most of the fund will be invested in equities or equity related products. PPF will be invested in accordance with the small savings scheme rules. NPS will be invested in line with the pension scheme options. The NPS is designed to be a retirement scheme and has rules on how you can access the money.

The structure of the products is quite different. Mutual funds will see the NAV fluctuating. PPF will earn interest in accordance with the scheme rules. NPS will depend on the investment performance and charges. The reader shouldn’t see this as just variations of the same account.

## Liquidity

ELSS have lock-in periods in place for the investments made. Once the lock-in period has completed it can be redeemed in accordance with the scheme rules. The next SIP instalment will have a separate lock-in period. The exact details will need to be confirmed with the latest SEBI and scheme documents.

PPF has a long term period with specific rules around loans, partial withdrawals and maturity. Its not meant to be accessed daily. NPS has different rules around partial withdrawals and exits. Tier I is for retirement so has different partial withdrawal and exit rules. Tier II has different rules. Its not about which product can be accessed first. The household should consider when the money may be needed. Can it be withdrawn? What would be the value/tax implications? Emergency funds shouldn’t be locked away without having some fund that can be accessed.

## Investment Risk

ELSS has exposure to the equity market. The value of the investment can go up and down. Even though there is a lock-in period there is no assurance that the value won’t go down. PPF has different rules but there could be changes to the interest rate. This will be done via government notification. PPF has a long term period so can cause issues with liquidity. NPS has exposure to investment risk and has rules in place for when you can access the savings.

There are several factors to consider when looking at risk. This could be volatility, uncertainty, loss of buying power from inflation or not being able to access the funds when required. There may be little volatility with a product but not suitable for the goal due to the limited access. Some products could have more volatility but have a different role in a long term portfolio. The category of the product won’t give all the answers.

## Tax implications

Each product has different tax implications. This will depend on the tax year, product rules, contribution, eligibility and tax regime chosen. Older articles may mention rules from the Income-tax Act, 1961 but for tax years starting from 1 April 2026 tax will be in accordance with the Income-tax Act, 2025. Ensure you check the official tax portal and current law.

If the product has tax deductions this will reduce your taxable income. It will not be risk free. Tax benefits may not be available in all tax regimes. Just because it has a tax free withdrawal in certain scenarios doesn’t mean it is tax free for all withdrawals.

## Costs and documents

For ELSS ensure you look at the scheme information document. Key Information Memorandum, expense ratio, portfolio details, riskometer and redemption rules. Costs will impact your experience. Different schemes will invest differently. Don’t choose a scheme based on the category having tax features.

For PPF ensure you check the latest scheme rules. The bank/post office will have instructions on how to operate. For NPS ensure you check the subscriber model. Investment options, charges, partial withdrawal rules and exit rules. Ensure you keep a record of statements and contributions.

## Example scenario

You may have three different goals. Money that could be needed for a medical emergency, money that is going to be invested for a long term wealth goal and money that is being saved for retirement. In this example the emergency fund will need to be easily accessible. The market linked mutual fund may have volatility. The retirement fund may have exit rules. The small savings scheme may have a long term period.

You could have different requirements at different stages. A long term product can be useful for saving but may not be suitable for a short term payment. The key thing to remember is that just because something has a tax label doesn’t mean it can be used for all financial goals.

## Common misconceptions

ELSS is not a guaranteed return product. You shouldn’t assume that the interest rate will stay the same for PPF. NPS is not a bank account that can be accessed. Tax implications for the contributions and withdrawals will be different. These reasons are why you should look at the main documents.

## Conclusion

Each product has a different legal structure, exposure, access and tax implications. Comparing these can be useful. Ensure you verify the current scheme rules and tax implications. Particularly the Tax Year 2026-27. Consider why the money may be needed. This article is purely for educational purposes and does not recommend investing in any option.

## Checking the main documents

ELSS – Scheme information document, Key Information Memorandum, Portfolio details and costs. PPF – Latest government scheme documents and bank/post office instructions. NPS – Subscriber model and relevant PFRDA regulations and FAQs. A comparison can help with organising your questions but shouldn’t override the documents.

Note the dates of the rules. This is particularly useful for tax, contribution limits, lock-in rules and withdrawals. When updating this article note if the change impacts existing accounts, new accounts or certain transactions. Instead of saying ‘the rules have changed’ it is more useful to state what has changed.

## Tax deduction

A tax deduction will reduce your taxable income. This will only apply if you’re eligible and making an eligible contribution. The tax saving will depend on your taxable income, tax regime and current law. Calculate it separately from the risk and expected cash flows.

## How to responsibly use a comparison table

Ensure you note the date of the facts. Not everything will be fixed. ELSS has market risk whereas PPF will have interest in line with the notified rates. NPS could have different exit rules depending on the subscriber type and circumstances. A column for return can hide these differences. If there is a figure note what the period is, source and assumptions. If there isn’t a figure then state what the structure is.

Ensure you check if the tax statement applies to the contribution, growth or withdrawal. These are separate events and could have different rules. If an article states a product is tax free without stating the circumstances or taxpayer it is too vague and unreliable. Always link to the current official law. Note that the tax year will impact the tax.

## Different rules depending on account or transaction date

Assuming everything has the same tax implications can lead to inaccuracies. An ELSS purchase may have different rules depending on the tax period. PPF and NPS will have different rules for the account and transactions. Ensure you note the date and type of transaction. If there isn’t a clear answer to any special cases from an official source then note to verify.
